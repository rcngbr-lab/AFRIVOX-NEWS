# Market and audience assessment

**Status:** evidence plan and strategic hypotheses. No readership, revenue, market-share or willingness-to-pay claim in this document has been verified. Current demographic, connectivity, competitor, advertising and price facts require cited primary/current research and are marked `BLOCKED_EXTERNAL` where relied upon.

## 1. Audience problem hypotheses

AFRIVOX NEWS should test whether readers need:

- Timely Kinyarwanda reporting that provides context rather than copied headlines.
- Clear explanation of policy, public services, business, technology and regional developments.
- Traceable source references and visible correction practices.
- Mobile pages that work on modest devices and constrained data budgets.
- Carefully translated English/French versions for diaspora/institutional use.
- Trustworthy fact-checks and explainers separated from opinion and advertising.

These are discovery questions. They are not proof of a market.

## 2. Segment hypotheses

| Segment | Job to be done | Initial product hypothesis | Monetization hypothesis | Validation method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General Kinyarwanda readers | Understand important local developments | Fast news + concise context | Mostly advertising/sponsorship | Mobile interviews, return visits, topic tests |
| Professionals/SMEs | Interpret policy, markets and operating environment | Morning/business briefing | Direct sponsor, institutional access | Problem interviews, briefing pilot |
| Students/educators | Accessible current-affairs explanation | Explainers, fact-checks, archive | Institutional partnership—not assumed | School/university interviews |
| Diaspora | Reliable Rwanda context and language choice | Weekly bilingual digest | Support/membership hypothesis | Landing-page and interview test |
| Researchers/policy users | Cited, searchable historical reporting | Data stories, source links, archive | Licensing/institutional hypothesis | Workflow and procurement interviews |
| Sports/culture audiences | Timely specialist coverage | Dedicated feeds only if staffed | Sponsorship hypothesis | Limited series, repeat-use measurement |
| Premium-investigation readers | Accountability reporting | Long-form dossiers and briefings | Membership/grants hypothesis | Trust and willingness-to-pay research |
| Advertisers | Credible, measurable brand-safe reach | Direct packages with delivery report | Campaign fees | Buyer interviews and paid pilot |

No segment should receive a full permanent desk before staffing and demand evidence exist.

## 3. Geographic priority

1. **Primary:** Rwanda, Kinyarwanda-first mobile readers.
2. **Secondary:** East Africa/Africa topics with direct relevance and reporting capacity.
3. **Selective international/diaspora:** English/French translation and contextual briefings.

“Global reach” is a distribution possibility, not an initial editorial obligation. International expansion must not dilute the primary market or create unverified translation volume.

## 4. Competitive research framework

Research current Rwanda, regional and global comparators using public evidence and ethical observation. For each comparator record:

- Ownership and public editorial positioning.
- Language and priority audiences.
- Original reporting versus aggregation.
- Cadence and section depth.
- Direct, search, email, social, video and audio distribution.
- Advertising, subscription, event or institutional products.
- Correction/source transparency.
- Mobile performance and accessibility.
- Differentiating strengths and likely cost drivers.

Do not reproduce a competitor’s protected design, articles, images, database or subscriber material. Competitor traffic/revenue estimates must be attributed and treated as estimates.

## 5. Research instruments

### Reader interview prompts

1. Describe the last important news item you tried to understand.
2. Where did you first see it, and where did you verify it?
3. What made the item trustworthy or doubtful?
4. Which language/format did you prefer and why?
5. What prevented you from completing or returning to the article?
6. Which newsletter/notification frequency would be useful rather than intrusive?
7. Have you ever paid for news or supported journalism? What value justified it?
8. What personal information would you refuse to provide?

### Advertiser interview prompts

1. What were the last three campaign objectives and channels?
2. Who approves vendor, creative, contract and payment?
3. Which measurements are required, and how are they verified?
4. What payment terms and documentation are standard?
5. Which content/categories are unacceptable for brand safety?
6. Would the buyer pay for a bounded pilot? Interest alone is not a commitment.

## 6. Experiments and decision log

| Experiment | Duration | Success measure selected in advance | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Three-desk editorial pilot | 4–8 weeks | Cadence, return use, qualitative trust, correction rate | Maintain/reduce/expand desks |
| Daily vs weekly newsletter | 4–6 sends after consent setup | Verified opt-in, open/click definitions, unsubscribe/complaint rate | Choose sustainable cadence |
| Business briefing | 4 weeks | Repeat professional readers, interviews, sponsor/institutional interest | Continue or stop |
| Short video vs article explainer | 8–12 matched topics | Completion, site referral, production hours | Allocate format budget |
| Direct-ad pilot | One signed campaign | On-time approval/delivery, valid impressions, payment/receivable days | Refine or stop offer |
| Membership proposition | Interviews + nonbinding price page | Qualified intent and objections; no false checkout | Build only after payment/legal readiness |
| Print preorder concept | Separate study | Paid or contractually reliable demand, distribution/returns economics | Print pilot or `NO-GO` |

## 7. Audience strategy

### Acquire

- Search-ready original reporting and evergreen explainers.
- Responsible social/video formats that lead to the owned article.
- Partnerships with disclosed conflicts and measurable referral terms.
- Live/special coverage only when a staffed verification desk exists.

### Retain

- Consistent beats and publication times.
- Consent-based newsletters and language/topic preferences.
- Visible corrections, author profiles and public sources.
- Fast mobile pages, low-data media choices and RSS.

### Convert

- Newsletter verification first; reader registration only where it adds value.
- Membership/support after trust and willingness-to-pay evidence.
- Institutional products tied to a defined workflow, not a generic paywall.

### Protect

- Minimize personal data and prohibit forced registration for ordinary news unless approved.
- Measure only defined, lawful metrics.
- Never treat sensitive reader/source data as ad targeting inventory.

## 8. Measurement dictionary

| Metric | Definition requirement | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Page view | Valid first-party page event after bot/filter rules | Equating views with people or trust |
| Active reader | Documented time-window and activity rule | Inflating from background tabs |
| Returning reader | Same privacy-respecting identifier in defined period | Claiming identity across devices without consent |
| Engagement | Explicit depth/time/action definition | One opaque “engagement score” |
| Newsletter subscriber | Verified, non-suppressed address | Counting pending/unsubscribed addresses |
| Campaign impression | Defined viewability/bot rules | Billing raw requests as guaranteed humans |
| Conversion | Named action and denominator | Mixing sign-ups, paid members and clicks |
| Revenue | Earned/recognized under finance policy | Counting pipeline or unpaid proposals |

## 9. Evidence required before scale

- Current primary research and sample notes.
- Cited primary/current market and legal sources.
- Real content-production capacity and cost per desk/format.
- Signed advertiser/institutional evidence, not verbal enthusiasm.
- Privacy-safe analytics definitions and data-quality checks.
- Monthly cohort, cash and receivable review.

Until then, market expansion and physical print are `BLOCKED_EXTERNAL` or experimental.
