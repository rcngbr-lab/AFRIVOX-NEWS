# AFRIVOX NEWS Phase 0 media business plan

**Status:** strategic planning baseline; not evidence of incorporation, licensing, customer commitments or financing.  
**Default currency:** Rwandan francs (RWF).  
**Decision:** `CONDITIONAL GO` for a narrow digital-first pilot; `NO-GO` for owned print production and a full studio until demand and unit economics are independently validated.

## 1. Mission and editorial proposition

AFRIVOX NEWS should earn repeat use through accurate Kinyarwanda-first reporting, transparent sourcing, useful explanation and visible corrections. The defensible proposition is not “more headlines”; it is a disciplined newsroom that makes complex national, regional, business, technology and public-interest subjects understandable without sacrificing verification.

Launch proposition:

- Mobile-first, low-bandwidth Kinyarwanda reporting with selective English/French translations after human approval.
- Original national/public-interest, business/economy, technology and explainer/fact-check work rather than thin duplication of every category.
- Clear separation of reporting, opinion and paid content.
- Direct reader relationship through website, RSS and consent-based email rather than exclusive dependence on social platforms.
- Confidential-source handling, correction history and media-rights governance as trust features.

The proposition must be tested with readers and advertisers. It is a hypothesis, not proven demand.

## 3. Operating model

AFRIVOX NEWS should start as a modular, digital-first newsroom with four control domains:

1. **Editorial:** pitches, assignments, reporting, specialist checks, approval, publication and corrections.
2. **Audience:** distribution, reader feedback, newsletters and privacy-aware measurement.
3. **Commercial/finance:** advertiser pipeline, contracts, campaign operations, invoicing, payment recording and reconciliation.
4. **Technology/administration:** accounts, security, backups, monitoring and system configuration—without editorial publication power.

### Daily operating rhythm

- Morning news conference: coverage priorities, assignments, risks, costs and deadlines.
- Midday desk review: source/evidence gaps, media status and breaking-news changes.
- Pre-publication review: specialist gates and independent publishing authority.
- End-of-shift handover: unfinished work, embargoes, source-security constraints and scheduled items.
- Finance/commercial handover remains separate from the editorial news budget.

Twenty-four-hour operations should not be advertised until actual shifts and escalation coverage exist.

## 4. Revenue model and controls

Potential revenue streams, in validation order:

1. Direct display campaigns and clearly disclosed sponsorships.
2. Newsletter sponsorship after consent-based distribution operates reliably.
3. Events/training with conflict-of-interest review and event-level budgets.
4. Reader membership/support after a willingness-to-pay experiment.
5. Institutional subscriptions or research briefs when a distinctive product exists.
6. Syndication/content licensing under written rights agreements.
7. Physical edition sales only after circulation validation.

Required controls:

- A sales lead is not revenue. Forecast only signed contracts at an approved probability.
- Editorial staff do not promise favorable coverage; commercial staff cannot publish news.
- Sponsored material is labeled and passes legal/advertising review.
- Invoice creation, payment recording, refunds and reconciliation should be separated as staffing permits.
- Revenue concentration, accounts-receivable age, delivery evidence and campaign credits are reviewed monthly.

## 5. Customer and audience validation plan

### Reader discovery

- Conduct 30–45 structured interviews across general readers, professionals, students, diaspora and researchers.
- Run a four-week Kinyarwanda reporting pilot across three to five supported desks.
- Measure direct visits, return frequency, article completion proxy, newsletter opt-in and qualitative trust—not follower count alone.
- Conduct usability sessions on low-cost Android devices and constrained networks.

### Commercial discovery

- Interview 12–20 prospective advertisers/agencies about actual approval cycles, formats, evidence, payment terms and prior campaign budgets.
- Test a documented pilot rate card without representing interest as bookings.
- Require signed order/contract, creative approval and invoicing terms before recognizing a campaign.

### Phase thresholds to be approved before the pilot

Targets must be set by the board/editorial/commercial leads before measurement. Suggested categories—not predetermined numbers—are: weekly return rate, newsletter verification rate, publishing cadence achieved without bypass, correction rate, sales conversion, receivable days and advertiser concentration.

## 6. Twelve-month execution

| Period | Focus | Evidence required to continue |
|---|---|---|
| Months 0–2 | Legal/business setup, secure staging, hiring core leads, audience interviews | Named accountable officers; staging acceptance; approved policies |
| Months 2–4 | Three-to-five-desk editorial pilot, direct audience capture, sales discovery | Reliable cadence, documented verification, real reader feedback |
| Months 4–6 | Limited direct campaigns/newsletter after controls | Signed contracts, delivery logs, consent records, reconciled payments |
| Months 6–9 | Improve retention, business desk, selective multimedia | Repeat use, production capacity and rights/storage evidence |
| Months 9–12 | Evaluate membership, institutional products and fixed digital edition | Measured willingness to pay and positive contribution-margin scenario |

Physical print requires a separate board decision and must not be funded from assumed digital growth.

## 7. Principal risks

| Risk | Early indicator | Mitigation / stop rule |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient original reporting capacity | Missed deadlines, rewritten releases | Reduce desks; protect reporting budget; stop expansion |
| Audience depends on one platform | High social reach, weak direct return | Newsletter/RSS/direct habit experiments; diversify distribution |
| Advertising compromises independence | Requests for coverage control | Written firewall; refuse or terminate noncompliant campaign |
| Cash shortfall/late receivables | Rising unpaid invoices | Deposits/credit limits; weekly cash forecast; hiring gates |
| Source or account compromise | Unexplained access/audit events | Incident response, session revocation, source-safety escalation |
| Legal/compliance gap | Missing registrations/policies/contracts | `BLOCKED_EXTERNAL`; no public launch/affected product |
| Shared-host constraints | Slow jobs, upload failures, outages | Move media/jobs to suitable infrastructure at measured trigger |
| Print losses | High returns or unsigned distribution | Keep print inactive; outsource pilot only after unit economics |

## 8. Investment recommendation

### Digital pilot: `CONDITIONAL GO`

Proceed only with:

- Verified legal/company/media readiness.
- Editor-in-Chief or equivalent accountable editorial leader.
- At least one independent review/publishing route and fact/legal access.
- Six months of approved cash runway under the chosen staffing scenario.
- Secure staging, backup/restore exercise and incident ownership.
- Measured audience and advertiser validation milestones.

### Multimedia expansion: `CONDITIONAL GO AFTER VALIDATION`

Approve per-format pilots only when staff, rights, storage, accessibility and distribution capacity are funded.

### Physical newspaper: `NO-GO PENDING SEPARATE STUDY`

Do not activate until paid demand, printer specifications/quotes, delivery partners, return rates and working capital are documented.

## 9. External decisions

`BLOCKED_EXTERNAL`: incorporation/ownership, current media/data/tax/employment obligations, licenses/registrations, banking/payment accounts, insurance, legal counsel, supplier contracts, production hosting, mail/scanner/monitoring services, staff compensation, premises and current supplier prices.
